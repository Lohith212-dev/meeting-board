# How to analyse a meeting

The board exists so that someone who missed a 2-hour call knows, in 10 minutes: what was decided, what they must do, what is still open, and which 5-10 minutes of the recording they should watch.

## 1. Chapters (read everything first)
- Read the full transcript once without writing. Then mark where the topic changes. A new topic usually starts with a question, "now", "one more thing", a screen share, or a new project name.
- 4-8 chapters. Each gets a title a colleague would use ("Pricing: starter tier or usage add-on?"), start/end seconds, a project, and 3 "what you'll see" lines.
- When someone reads out an AI or document aloud, add a `note` ("The CEO reads an AI draft aloud in this part") so readers know it is a sample, not agreement.

## 2. Summary: Minto pyramid
- `lead`: the governing thought, the answer first, under 10 words ("Ship Insights small, price it with evidence").
- `accent`: the one sentence that makes the lead concrete.
- 4 `points`: the supporting outcomes, one per major thread, each a 3-5 word title + 1-2 sentences.
- Test: could someone retell the meeting from these 5 lines?

## 3. Pick a picture per discussion (frameworks behind the blocks)
| What the discussion looked like | Block | Framework |
|---|---|---|
| "The user goes from A to B to C, and at each step…" | `steps` | Customer journey |
| "First this happens, then the system does…" | `flow` | Process / issue tree |
| "Two people want different things" | `conflict` | Conflict Resolution Diagram (shared goal, two positions, the need behind each, way forward) |
| "Week one… week two… then we scale" | `columns` | Phased plan with gates |
| "We need three things from the customer" | `cards` | Concept list |
| "Our idea vs theirs" | `pair` | Comparison |
| "Who to target, when, with what, measured how" | `tiles` | Fact sheet |
| How projects relate to each other | concept map (`map`, `links`) | Concept map |

Put the strongest quote of the discussion in the block's `quote` with its timestamp.

## 4. Decisions vs proposals (Ladder of inference)
- A decision needs someone with authority agreeing out loud. Write the reason they gave (`why`).
- An idea someone floated, or a plan an AI drafted and read out, is `Proposed`, not `Agreed`.
- Facts heard vs assumptions: if the meeting assumed it, say "assumed" in `why`.

## 5. Open questions (Cynefin)
For each unresolved point: why it's open, what would settle it, who, next step, and `kind`:
- `Research`: the answer exists but nobody has it yet.
- `Small test`: complex; only trying it will tell (run a pilot, measure).
- `Expert`: complicated; someone who knows can answer.
- `Decision`: options are clear; someone just has to choose.

## 6. Tasks (Eisenhower)
- Verb first, one outcome per task, owner(s) as said in the meeting.
- `now` = urgent and important (has a near date or blocks others). `next` = important, not urgent. `proposed` = from a draft plan nobody committed to yet.
- `top: true` on the 3-4 that matter most this week; they go on the overview.
- External people (partners, clients) get `external: true` (keep "our to-dos" and "client to-dos" apart).

## 7. Watch list (who should watch what)
- Only people who were not on the call, or who joined late or left early (for the part they missed).
- Use `team.json → cares_about` and their role to choose parts. 1-3 ranges each, each 1-6 minutes, each with a reason in their language ("Week 2: your team answers questions").
- Target: under 10 minutes per person. Say the total meeting length on the board so the saving is visible.

## 8. The understanding check (quiz)
- 5-7 questions, one per thing people must not get wrong: the main decision, owners and dates, how a plan works, what is still open, who the customer is.
- Wrong options must be plausible (things that were discussed but not decided, other people's names, other dates). Never trick questions.
- `why`: one line that teaches the answer. `review`: the tab (and project) plus the timestamp range that answers it, so a wrong answer sends people straight to the right place.
- Pass mark: all but one.

## 9. Spelling check
- Look for: product and company names, people's names, acronyms (ICP, CRM), technical words (observability), UK/US spelling from auto-captions (draught, cheque).
- `sure: true` only when there is no doubt. Everything else goes to the user to confirm.

## 10. Writing style
Plain English, short sentences, numbers as digits on screen, no em dashes, no jargon. Name things the way the team names them. Never pad: if a section has nothing, leave it out.
